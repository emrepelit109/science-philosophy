The Fundamental Difference Between a QPU and the Heart

Upon further reflection, I realized that the idea I mentioned in my previous article, Building a Human from Scratch—where a quantum processing unit (QPU) is modeled as the heart and interacts with the CPU in the head via digital-to-analog converters within the body to play a role in the decision-making process—is incomplete. In fact, if we want to transfer an exact human model to a robot, we must consider that humans do not consist solely of mind and heart; just as migratory birds navigate without losing their way through a protein in their bodies (Cryptochrome 4), humans also possess many auxiliary elements.

Throughout history, whenever people could not find a way to advance the scientific development of their era, they assumed that scientific knowledge had reached its absolute maximum. However, because human biology is such a complex structure, it will never be fully understood. In short, humans also have molecular structures whose functional aspects are largely unknown, and we do not know which of these unknown molecular structures bestows the function of willpower. Consequently, we cannot know what kind of enhancements to use to grant willpower to a robot. Therefore, building humans and other living beings from scratch is impossible. We only simulate these structures and fool ourselves into thinking we are producing their imitations.

As I stated in my previous article, a human has no access to the mind of any other being, which is why every person is fundamentally condemned to live a solitary life. Therefore, the model in which the Creator brings a different universe into existence for each human, designing everyone else they perceive merely as an environmental variable for that person, is quite consistent with reality. People should focus on self-actualization.
