The Ease of Creating a Perfect Human Compared to Creating an Imperfect Human and the Proof of the Creator

Many people might wonder: If there is a Creator, why did He not create His creations perfect, but instead created them as beings that make mistakes and ruin everything? Doesn't this cast a shadow on the magnificence of the Creator? Let us read this question in reverse and consider it together with today's computer science technology.

Do you think it is easier to make a robot that makes mistakes and then regrets them, or to make a robot that executes what is written in a strict, step-by-step algorithm? Or, if it is harder to create something without flaws, why did we, after the Industrial Revolution, first create the "harder" one—that is, the one that strictly follows an algorithm—while only today, through quantum processors, have we developed robots that can think, make decisions, question their own decisions, and learn and apply on their own?

The answer to the question is simple, isn't it? In fact, the Creator created the human as the most complex organism, and created all other living things as tools connected to each other with a bond of cosmic interconnectedness, like the cogs of a machine. Thus, any change at any point in the universe has affected the entire universe. The human heart can be thought of as a quantum processor, and the human brain as the main processor. Actually, in my previously written article, "Building a Human from Scratch," I explained from my own perspective what decision-making mechanisms the Creator might have used to create the human. Those who wish can take a look at that as well.

There is much more to tell on this subject, but I hope to detail all of it in my future articles.
