 Time and Thought-Provoking Questions





As I have stated in my previous blog posts, since the concept of time is not fully understood, we cannot know how many dimensions spacetime actually possesses. In those earlier posts, we touched upon the possibility that time might be composed of infinitesimally small particles called chronons.


But what if time actually surrounds us entirely—much like the condition of a fish swimming in the sea—and can be modeled as a fluid, unidirectional quantity, just like the flow of an ocean? In such a model, wouldn't it be plausible that to rise to the ocean's surface and observe the external environment, we could generate vortices (akin to a tsunami forming in that very ocean) and break through to the surface via centrifugal force?


Furthermore, what kind of environment awaits us outside this ocean of time? Could the belief in the afterlife (ahiret) within the philosophy of religion be interpreted this way, or is this realm identical to the place humans are believed to go after death? What is the connection between the moment of death and the vortex I mentioned earlier? Could the state a human experiences at the moment of death be a form of turmoil and chaos?


Let this be enough for such abstract questions and answers in which one could easily get lost; if you wish, let us move on to more logical explanations.

Since we cannot fully unravel the mystery of the concept of time, there is no possibility of knowing exactly how many dimensions the spacetime continuum has. Even if time consists of particles called chronons, because none of us can directly observe them, we cannot fully decipher the temporal dimension.


If time is indeed an ocean in which the universe sways to and fro, the only way for us to rise to the surface and observe the outside realm would be to accelerate a massive object to the speed of light. This would generate the necessary centrifugal force to trigger a vortex. Although today's technology does not permit this, quantum teleportation experiments at CERN have shown that massive particles moving close to the speed of light can indeed be teleported.


This leads us to the thesis that the universe is an entity swimming in the ocean of time, and objects reaching near-light speeds can create a vortex, thereby exiting this temporal ocean. As those familiar with physics know, centrifugal force depends on both mass and velocity. From this perspective, if an object with a much larger mass than the quantum particles at CERN moves even at a speed well below the speed of light (though still significantly fast), it could still be teleported or, in a sense, exit the ocean of time.

A similar kind of chaos might prevail at the moment of death, thus allowing an exit from the ocean of time. In an environment of turmoil, components move much faster than they normally do, and outside the ocean of time, an afterlife environment—in line with religious beliefs—may well exist. This is entirely possible.
